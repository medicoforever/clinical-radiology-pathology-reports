### Case Report #1 (BEFUND_UBEID: `51116`)
**Modality Context:** `X-ray / MRI`  

```text
MRI SCAN ANKLE

Technique: T1, T2, PD, IR sagittal, axial, coronal, T1 contrast

Ankle joint alignment is normal.

A well-defined slightly lobulated eccentric expansile lytic lesion measuring approximately 2.2 X 2.2 X 1.2 cm (CC X AP X TRA) is seen in the inferomedial aspect of the calcaneum in the posterior part. No obvious sclerotic rim is seen in the medial aspect of the lesion. Few small foci of deossification of the cortex are seen in the medial and posterior aspects.  However no evidence of significant extension beyond the bone seen in the present study.  

The lesion appears heterogeneously hypo to isointense on T1 and hyperintense on T2/IR sequences.  The lesion contains solid and cystic components. The cystic areas contain fluid fluid levels.

On contrast administration, there is significant enhancement of the soft tissue component and thin enhancement of the rim of the cystic areas. Mild oedema is seen in the adjacent region of the calcaneum. Rest of the bones and soft tissue appear grossly normal.

Mild joint effusion is seen.  Fluid is seen in the flexor hallucis longus tendon. No abnormal enhancement of the synovium seen.

Rests of the bones show no alteration in signal intensity. Small Bone Island is seen in the anterior aspect of the calcaneum.

Joint space and articular cartilage appear normal. Visualized ligaments appear grossly normal.

There is no evidence of significant soft tissue swelling.

IMPRESSION:

Well-defined lytic lesion with solid and cystic components in the calcaneum as detailed above. May represent giant cell tumor.
Mild joint effusion. 
Suggested HPE correlation.
```

### Case Report #2 (BEFUND_UBEID: `78514`)
**Modality Context:** `X-ray / MRI`  

```text
ULTRASOUND SCAN OF LEFT ANKLE

Clinical profile: No h/o significant trauma.
The tibialis anterior tendon is thickened and shows moderate fluid collection around it. The maximum thickened portion of tendon measure 1.0 x 1.0 cm (AP x TR) in its mid-portion.  The tendon shows mild vascularity within it. Small isoechoic well defined component of size 1.4 x 1.0 x 0.7 cm is also seen within this fluid abutting the tendon, laterally.  No obvious vascularity/calcifications is seen within it.
Distal portion of tibial anterior tendon appears normal.
Rests of the tendons around ankle joint appear normal.
No obvious joint effusion is seen.
Anterior tibial vessels and dorsalis paedis artery show normal flow.
Subcutaneous tissue shows mild edema.

Impression: 
"	Thickened tibialis anterior tendon with fluid and isoechoic component along it as detailed, may represent ganglion cyst/giant cell tumor of tendon-- Needs further evaluation with MRI study.
```

### Case Report #3 (BEFUND_UBEID: `192797`)
**Modality Context:** `X-ray / MRI`  

```text
CT scan of right humerus:  

Clinical details:  Patient presenting with pain in the right shoulder for past 15 days with the radiograph showing a lytic lesion involving the humerus.

There is a lytic lesion involving the epiphysis and metaphysis of the upper end of the right humerus.  The lesion is filled by soft tissue.

No significant expansion of the bone noted.  Craniocaudally, the lesion extends for a length of 7.3 cm.

No obvious cystic areas noted within. No evidence of any matter is within the lesion.  
No evidence of periosteal reaction.

There is variable thinning of the cortex with few areas showing cortical break.

The contour of the head of the humerus however appears normal.

There is clear demarcation between the lower aspect of the lesion and the adjacent bone marrow.

Loss of fat plane between the lesion and the adjacent muscles in the lower aspect.

Rest of the visualised bones are normal.

Impression:  
Lytic lesion involving the proximal end of the humerus as detailed - possibility of giant cell

tumor to be considered.
Suggested HPE correlation.
```
