### Case Report #1 (BEFUND_UBEID: `20631`)
**Modality Context:** `HRCT Temporal Bone / MRI`  

```text
C.T.SCAN OF BRAIN - PLAIN AND CONTRAST

Serial axial sections of the brain were studied from the base to the vertex.
The patient is known case of the right intracerebellar abscess with obstructive hydrocephalous.

Small hypodense area measuring 1.5 X 0.9 x 1.2 cm (ap x cc x tr) size is noted involving the posterior aspect of the right cerebellar hemisphere.
Small extraaxial hypodense collection is noted in the posterior and inferior aspect of right cerebellar hemisphere measuring 2.0 x 0.7 x 2.4 cm (cc x ap x tr) and 2.8 x 0.9 x 2.5 cm (ap x cc x tr) respectively.
Minimal peripheral enhancement is noted. Mass effect is noted over the fourth ventricle and brainstem.
Small hypodense area is noted involving the lateral aspect of right cerebellar hemisphere - represent small infarct.
The lateral and third ventricle is dilated.  Ventricular hemispheric ratio measures 4.0:9.7 and third ventricle diameter measures 1 cm. Periventricular seepage of CSF noted along the occipital horn. 

Filling defect is noted within the right transverse sinus - suggestive of thrombosis. Rests of the dural venous sinuses are normal. Intracranial arteries are normal.

Rest of the brain parenchyma shows no altered signal density.

There is no midline shift seen.

The cortical sulci and sylvian fissures are normal.

The sella, parasellar and suprasellar regions are normal.

Soft tissue density noted within the right middle ear cavity and right mastoid air cells with the erosion of the right ossicular chain.

IMPRESSION: 

The patient is a known case of the right intracerebellar abscess with obstructive hydrocephalous.

1)	Present scan shows small hypodense area involving the right cerebral hemisphere - may represent abscess.
2)	Subdural empyema involving the posterior inferior aspect of the right cerebellar region with mass effect over the fourth ventricle and the brainstem with obstructive hydrocephalous as detailed.
3)	Small infarct involving the right cerebellar hemisphere.
4)	Dural venous sinus thrombosis involving the right transverse sinus.
5)	Cholesteatoma involving the right middle ear and aditus ad antrum.

Dr.lakshmanan
```
