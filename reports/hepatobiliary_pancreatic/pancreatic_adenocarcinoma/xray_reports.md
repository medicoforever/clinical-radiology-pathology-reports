INTRODUCTION

The term "thyroid nodule" refers to a distinct lesion within the thyroid gland that is palpably or radiologically distinct from the surrounding thyroid parenchyma. Thyroid nodules are common, seen in about 8.5% of the population (1).

They are more common among women. In India the prevalence of a palpable thyroid nodule in the community is about 12.2%, according to a recent study (2)


Thyroid nodules are clinically important incidental findings with a reported prevalence rate ranging from 4.2% in the Framingham population-based study to 50% in a study at the Mayo clinic based on autopsy data (3,4)

Although in the general population,most thyroid nodules are benign, the prevalence of thyroid cancer is as high as 5%-10% (5)

However, thyroid cancer is quite rare, and the incidence is 8.7 per 100000 people per year, though this seems to be increasing over the years.(6)

The main aim of managing thyroid nodules is to reassure the majority of patients who have benign disease and to diagnose the minority of patients who will prove to have malignant disease. Conventional ultra-sonography can provide meaningful information in thyroid nodule diagnosis, however, there has been considerable variation in diagnostic performances.  (7)


As reported by Hippocrates, palpation is an ancient diagnostic technique used in several fields and especially for thyroid clinical evaluation. A firm or hard nodule consistency is associated with an increased risk of malignancy. One of the key features of thyroid gland cancer on palpation is the degree of firmness: malignant lesions tend to be much harder than benign ones (8)



The stiffness of thyroid nodules is dependent on the composition and cellularity of the nodule. A firm and hard thyroid nodule on palpation is associated with an increased risk of malignancy (7) 

Previous ex vivo and in vivo studies have documented significant differences in stiffness of normal thyroid tissue and tumors of the thyroid gland. (9)

However, palpation is subjective and highly dependent on the examiner and on the size and location of the nodules (10)


Conventional ultrasound provides information regarding characteristics correlated with the risk of cancer, such as hypoechogenicity, blurred or spiculated margins, microcalcifications, an anteroposterior/transverse diameter (A/T) of 1 cm or greater, and intranodular vascularity. However, the sensitivity, specificity, negative predictive value (NPV), and positive predictive value (PPV) for these criteria are extremely variable from study to study. However, no ultrasound feature has both high sensitivity and high specificity (11,12,13,14)


US imaging is a definitive method for detection of thyroid nodules; however, the predictive value of US in differentiation of malignant nodules is limited. Currently, the best available method for discrimination of malignant from benign thyroid nodules is fine needle aspiration biopsy (FNAB). Conventional ultrasound does not provide direct information corresponding to the hardness of a nodule. Ultrasound elastography was developed to obtain information on tissue stiffness noninvasively. US elastography has been successfully applied in the breast and more recently in the prostate gland (15,16)

Elastography has been introduced to evaluate the tissue hardness objectively and to augment the diagnostic accuracy of gray-scale ultrasonography (US) (17)

Elasticity imaging quantifies and displays tissue stiffness properties non-invasively by measuring their displacement in response to mechanical stimulation. The word elastography, first coined by Ophir et al. in 1991, has become an umbrella term for any elasticity imaging technique. Elastography is the science of creating noninvasiveimages of mechanical characteristics of tissues. (18)

Real time ultrasound elastography is an emerging technique. It estimates the tissue stiffness by measuring tissue deformation from returning ultrasonic signals before and after applying an external pressure. The firm tissues show less deformation than soft tissues with compression. The relative elasticity, i.e., strain, in the region of interest is calculated from its deformation rate. A firm stiffness is associated with an increased risk of malignancy in thyroid nodules. (19)
Using the same physical basis as clinical palpation, elastography has clinical usefulness because pathologic processes including cancer and fibrosis alter tissue elasticity (stiffness).  USE has been most extensively investigated in the breast where evidence suggests that it can improve the specificity of conventional US for breast malignancy without appreciably lowering its sensitivity  (20)

The basic concept of US elastography is that compression applied to the thyroidtissue produces the strain (tissue displacement in longitudinal direction) within the tissue, and the amount of strain is less in harder tissues than in softer ones. Elastography is usefulin differentiating malignant from benign thyroid nodules because malignant nodules are harder than the surrounding adjacent parenchyma (21)

REVIEW OF LITERATURE:

The management of thyroid nodules is of clinical importance due to the relatively high incidence of malignant disease and the relative difficulty in distinguishing benign from malignant nodules by conventional sonographic imaging (22)

In India the prevalence of a palpable thyroid nodule in the community is about 12.2%, according to a recent study. They are more common among women. (23)

The challenge in evaluating thyroid nodules is to detect thyroid cancer that occurs in 5-10% depending on age, gender, history of radiation exposure, family history and other factors (24)


The American Thyroid Association (ATA) guidelines state that: "with the exception of suspicious cervical lymphadenopathy, which is a specific but insensitive finding, no single sonographic feature or combinations of features is adequately sensitive or specific to identify all malignant nodules". In addition to it, the document states that elastography is an emerging and promising sonographic technique that requires additional validation with prospective studies. (25)


Hence, whenever a patient presents with a thyroid swelling, the task of the clinician is to distinguish the benign nodule from the malignant one. This is a difficult task, and no test is perfect in this regard. However, a reasonable amount of success can be achieved by a good clinical evaluation and investigations.





Important causes of thyroid nodules:

 



The presence of the following factors in addition to the thyroid nodule should evoke further investigations: a history of radiation, male gender, extremes of age (<20 or >70 years), history of neck irradiation, nodule >4 cm in size or the presence of any pressure symptoms. (26)


Histologically, more than 90% of thyroid cancers are differentiated, comprising papillary and follicular carcinoma (27)

Conventional Ultrasonography:

Ultrasonography is the first choice and the most sensitive imaging modality for diagnosing intrathyroid lesions. Because of its superficial location, the thyroid gland is ideally suited for high-frequency sonography (using 7-13 MHz transducer) which facilitates the detection of clinically non-palpable nodules of 2-3 mm size and allows a more accurate morphological characterization of the lesion. It is also used to determine the size and number of thyroid nodules, to assess the volume of thyroid tissue in cases of thyromegaly, and to differentiate thyroid masses from adjacent non-thyroid masses.

Computed tomography (CT) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI):

These modalities have an adjuvant role in the evaluation of thyroid disease. CT and MRI are less sensitive than USG in detecting and characterizing intrathyroid lesions as benign and malignant. They are particularly used for staging thyroid cancer as they are useful in evaluating regional lymphadenopathy, assessing loco-regional extension of the tumor (particularly involving trachea and esophagus), spread of disease into the mediastinum or retrotracheal region and detecting pulmonary and hepatic metastases. CT and MR imaging are useful to evaluate the occult metastases (mediastinal or retrophayngeal) in post-thyroidectomy follow-up cases with elevated serum Tg level and negative sonographic finding.
CT is most sensitive in detecting intra-glandular calcification. Diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) may be helpful in differentiating benign and malignant nodules. Benign nodules have higher apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) values than malignant ones. MR spectroscopy using long echo-time (TE) has been proved to be a sensitive method in differentiating thyroid carcinoma from benign follicular lesion. Choline peak is identified in almost all carcinomas, with raised choline/creatine ratio ranging from 1.6 in well differentiated carcinoma to 9.4 in anaplastic carcinoma. The normal thyroid tissue and benign follicular lesions generally demonstrate no choline peak.
However, both of these imaging modalities have almost no role in the initial evaluation of a thyroid nodule, and are rarely indicated in the initial workup.

Radionuclide imaging: 

Nuclear medicine plays an integral role in the management of differentiated thyroid cancer.  Nuclear scans use either one of the isotopes of iodine or technetium. These are handled differently by the follicular cells. Normal follicular cells take up both, but only radioiodine is organified and stored. Most benign and malignant neoplasms concentrate isotopes less avidly leading to a "cold" area on scanning. Ultrasound Elastography

Introduction:

Elastography is a recent technology in ultrasound that maps relative tissue stiffness. It examines the mechanical and elastic properties of soft tissue, which rely on the composition and structural organization of the macromolecules. Some pathologic conditions induce considerable changes in the soft tissue structure, modifying the elastic properties and leading to increased firmness and reduced mobility of the involved tissue. 
This modality depicts relative tissue stiffness or displacement (strain) in response to an imparted force (stress). Ultrasound elastography is based on the comparison of signals acquired before and after tissue displacement. Stiff tissues deform less and exhibit less strain than compliant tissues in response to the same applied force. Thus, the basis of elastography is analogous to manual palpation. 

Malignant thyroid nodules tend to be much harder than benign ones. Papillary thyroid carcinoma has complex papillae with a central fibrovascular stalk, psammoma bodies and fibrosis which result in increased firmness.

Several techniques have been devised in ultrasound elastography which includes compression strain imaging, vibrationelastography or shear wave, acoustic radiation force generated by the ultrasound pulse, and real-time shear velocity. Among these techniques, compression sonoelastography and shear-wave sonoelastography currently have the most prominent role. 

Compression elastography (Stress-strain elastography)
It is a qualitative type of elastography. Strain profile is calculated in a direction perpendicular to the tissue surface in response to an externally applied stress. The amount of tissue deformation after the application of stress which is the strain profile is calculated based on the inbuilt soft ware within the system. The amount deformation of the strain profile is mapped with the help of colour coding and the resultant image obtained is called an elastogram. On the images stiffer / harder areas are seen as dark and softer / elastic areas are white according to convention.  The colour-coding for the same can be as blue and green respectively based on the user configuration and the software which comes with the system. Thus ultrasound elastography helps in characterization of lesion which was otherwise isoechoic on ultrasound. The most important thing in obtaining the elastogram is optimal compression levels which is indicated by the side bar on the right side of the image. 
Strain elastography was the first technique to appear on clinical US systems. Transducer positioning is similar to that of conventional US, with identification of the tissue of interest in the gray-scale image. Then either gentle intermittent freehand compression
is applied via the transducer along the axis of the US beam (axial), or the transducer is held stationary and endogenous stimuli such as arterial pulsations or other physiologic motions are used to displace tissues.  From the radiofrequency (RF) data received, tissue displacements along the beam axis (axial) are determined by comparison of successive image frames using software cross-correlation methods, and tissue strain (change in length/original length) is calculated for each spatial location.
The resultant strain data are displayed graphically as a two-dimensional map of relative tissue strain, called an elastogram (Fig. 1). Elastograms are usually displayed as a semi-transparent overlay onto gray-scale images using a colour-coded scale. Nowadays, all commercial strain USE systems display strain elastograms dynamically, giving rise to the term real-time elastography (RTE).

Shear-wave elastography

Shear-wave elastography is a quantitative type of elastography. In this method shear waves are generated near the region of interest. The system monitors the propagation of shear waves in the region of interest using Doppler like ultrasound technique and it gives us the velocity of propagation. The shear wave velocity is calculated and displayed in meters per second or kilopascals using the young's modulus. Thus we can quantify the velocity of shear waves within the lesion using elastography. In SWE systems, a specially modified transducer produces focused impulses of high-intensity acoustic radiation (ultrasound), termed push pulses, at specific locations within the tissue of interest. This induces shear waves, whose velocities are tracked by a series of normal intensity impulses using ultrasound correlation methods(Fig. 2). FNAC:

Ultrasound-guided fine-needle aspiration (FNA), provides a cytology specimen for analysis, is currently the standard test to determine whether the thyroid nodule requires more aggressive treatment (e.g., surgical removal). It is useful tool for thyroid cancer diagnosis, especially in the case of papillary carcinoma. In follicular carcinoma, histopathology is required because the diagnosis is based on the presence or absence of capsular or vascular invasion.  However, US-guided FNAB is an invasive procedure, subject to sampling errors, and consumes time and economic resources, and it is associated with minor complications. It is preferred to limit its application for indeterminate nodules that may turn out benign. None of the B-mode ultrasound features are capable of detecting all cancerous nodules, and almost 70% of histologically proved benign nodules may have one of the suspicious B-mode ultrasound features. Therefore, the American Thyroid Association has recommended sonographic assessment of thyroid nodules before making the decision whether the patient should undergo FNA. The high incidence of nodules, the invasiveness of the procedure, and the possibility of sampling error require performing protocols for determining with relative confidence which nodules should be followed up and which should be aspirated.

MATERIAL & METHODS


All patients with thyroid nodules seen on B mode sonography are included. 
Then these patients are subjected to B-mode sonography and sonoelastography in the same sitting. Then the thyroid nodules are evaluated with histopathology / FNAC
All these patients will be examined with Voluson-E8 ultrasound equipment.
INCLUSION CRITERIA:
All patients presenting with thyroid nodule who underwent surgery or FNAC that are assessed with B-mode sonography will be included.
EXCLUSION CRITERIA: 
¢	Thyroid nodules with purely cystic component.
¢	Thyroid nodule with calcified shell.
¢	Thyroid nodules whose histopathological correlation not available.
¢	Thyroid nodules larger than probe width.
¢	Thyroid nodules with macrocalcifications (calcifications > 2mm) 



Sample size:
117 individual cases were studied. 
A similar study conducted by Farzana A. and colleagues, AJR:191, August 2008 included only 35 individual cases and they sampled five to six lymph nodes from every single patient. 

Statistical methods: 
1.	Descriptive statistics to enumerate the basic characteristics of the group on the basis of histopathology (reference standard).
2.	Specificity, sensitivity, positive predictive value, negative predictive value for B-mode ultrasound and sonoelastography separately in comparison with histopathology.

Time frame: from 1ST March 2014 to 1st December 2015. 
Method of procedure: 
All patients with thyroid nodules seen on B mode sonography were included. 
Patients were subjected for B-mode ultrasonography in supine position with pillow underneath the shoulders to extend the neck. B-mode characteristics of thyroid nodule were assessed.
The nodule was also evaluated for 
1.	Echogenicity : Hyperechoic, isoechoic, or hypoechoic with respect to normal thyroid parenchyma)
2. Calcifications:  no calcifications, microcalcifications (presence of hyperechoic  spots<2 mm with or without acoustic shadowing), coarse dense calcifications, or peripheral rim-like calcifications
3. Presence of vascularity and its pattern on Doppler: Absence ofblood flow, perinodular and absent intranodular blood flow or intranodular blood flow.
4. Perinodularhypoechoic halo.
5. Margins: Well defined and smooth, blurred, or spiculated. 

After detailed B-mode ultrasound examination, the thyroid nodules were evaluated on sonoelastography with a linear probe. The thyroid nodule was then compressed with the ultrasound probe and slowly released. The adequacy of compression was indicated by the side bar showing green color. Images were discarded when the side bar showed red or yellow color indicating inadequate compression. The sonoelastographic pattern of thyroid nodule after adequate compression was evaluated and categorized according to five groups as categorized above in table and color diagram (figure 5). The procedure was repeated at least once to avoid spurious result.
These patietns are evaluated by sono-elastography in the same setting and assigned Rago et al score according to stiffness depicted by color system.

During elastography, the probe was positioned perpendicular to the skin while applying pressure. Images were obtained by applying light repetitive compression at the skin above the targeted thyroid nodule. A square region of interest positioning the target nodule at the center of the box was set for elastography acquisition. Radiologists obtained optimal images showing both color homogeneity within the region of interest and pressure indicator displayed on the screen ranging between 2 and 3. Images were displayed in a split-screen mode, with the gray-scale images on the right and the translucent colorscaleelastography images superimposed on the corresponding gray-scale US image in the left.Each pixel of the elasticity image was shown as one of the 256 specific colors, representing the extent of strain. The scale ranged from red, showing areas of greatest strain (ie, softest component), to blue, showing no strain (ie, hardest component). 

Then the patients were followed for histopathology. 
The results of B-mode ultrasonography, sonoelastography and histopathology were compared with histopathology as reference standard.

DISCUSSION

The clinical challenge of managing thyroid nodules nowadays is to diagnose the minority of malignant disease. 
Among the available imaging modalities, diagnostic ultrasound is noninvasive and the most frequently used, to assess thyroid nodules. There are various criteria for characterization of thyroid nodules. During imaging, it is crucial to differentiate between benign and malignant thyroid nodules as the management and prognosis of these conditions are different.
Ultrasound Elastography is one of the recent advances in ultrasound technology. It is analogous to clinical palpation. A benign thyroid nodule maintains its relative softness. However malignant thyroid nodules are relatively stiffer. This relative elasticity of thyroid nodules  is assessed by the technique of ultrasound Elastography and the relative tissue elasticity is color mapped into five groups (figure 5) [3]. In this color map, blue indicates hard areas and indicates relative tissue hardness which favors malignancy.
To the best of our knowledge, this is one of the studies in India on accuracy of B-mode ultrasonography, elastography and in combination to differentiate benign from malignant thyroid nodules. Thus, no Indian population based study is available for comparison.

B-mode USG:
Since long, various parameters have been used based on B-mode ultrasound to differentiate benign versus malignant thyroid nodules. 
Echogenicity of the thyroid nodule: In our study, hypoechogenicity had 81.2% sensitivity and 76.2% specificity for detection of malignant thyroid nodules. This is in accordance with the study conducted by et al, who showed % sensitivity and % specificity.  Thus hypoechoic nodule favours the diagnosis of malignant etiology.
         With respect to margin, our study had lesser sensitivity and specificity ( 68.7% and 78.2% respectively) for irregular margins as a features to distinguish benign and malignant nodules. This suggests that for any given nodule appearance of margin cannot be relied on for differentiation of benign and malignant nodules.  
           The presence or absence of peri-nodular hypoechoic halo is an important criteria for the differential diagnosis of thyroid nodules.  In our study we found that this parameter had high sensitivity of 81.25%. This result is in accordance with the study conducted by and collegues who showed sensitivity of %.

This is in accordance with the results of study performed by et al , who showed specifity of % in their study. Most of the micro-calcifications were seen in cases of papillary carcinomas. Pathologically, micro-calcificaitons are caused by psammoma bodies, which are commonly seen in papillary carcinomas.  However, sensitivity of microcalcification for detection of malignant thyroid nodule was low (31.2%).  Micro-calcification may not be visible on ultrasound imaging due to poor calcium content. This could be one of the reason for low sensitivity in our study. Follicular carcinomas does not contain psammoma bodies and do not have micro-calcifications. In our study , about 23% of all malignant nodules were follicular carcinomas. This could be another reason for low sensitivity of micro-calcifications for detection of malignant thyroid nodules.

We have to realize that ultrasound elastography is not suitable for every patient, and it has some limitations. First, it must be performed with so-called high-end ultrasound devices rather than most conventional ultrasound devices. In addition, it is more expensive than conventional ultrasound equipment. With this technique's common application, it may become less costly in the future. Second, application of ultrasound elastography to nodules with calcification and predominant cystic lesions is not possible because the ultrasound beam does not cross the calcification, and probe compression does not result in tissue strain deformation, so artifacts within the color-coded image will be generated, which causes inaccurate information. Predominant cystic nodules and calcified nodules were excluded from most studies included in our meta- analysis

CONCLUSION

1.	Ultrasound elastography when combined with B-mode and color doppler gives an increased sensitivity in picking metastatic lymph nodes when compared to either modality alone.
2.	The specificity however is lower than that documented in studies from western population and thus a biopsy is a must. The variation is probably due to a significant number of patients having tuberculous cervical lymphadenopathy.
3.	ultrasound elastography has good diagnostic efficacy for differentiation of benign and malignant thyroid nodules. It can be used as a supplementary tool with grayscale sonography and can potentially reduce unnecessary fineneedle-aspiration biopsy
4.	US elastography is a relatively easy and quick-to-perform supplemental technique that may be practical for routine clinical care, mostly helping to identify probable benign nodules that could be followed up.
